Why it is different

The page is yours. So are the clients.

No commission

Not on a first booking, not on a hundredth. You pay the same whether you take £200 a week or £2,000.

Your own link

A page at your name, not a profile inside somebody else's app. Put it in your bio and it is the only thing she sees.

Your actual work

Your photographs next to the treatment they belong to. She books the set she can see, so she arrives wanting the right thing.

No rivals on your page

Marketplaces show your client three other techs nearby. Yours shows her you, and a time she can take.

They come back on their own

Three weeks after a full set she gets one message saying she is due, with a link to a time that is actually free. One tap, one opt-out, and never a second chase.

Wherever you work

A room at home, a rented chair, or the back of the car. Say which, and your page either shows your address and the parking or asks each client for hers.

The one thing that is not a convenience

A patch test your booking page actually enforces.

Tinting and lash extensions need one 24 to 48 hours beforehand. Your insurer sets the window, and if a client reacts and you cannot produce a dated record, the cover you pay for may not answer.

What every other booking page does

Sells the appointment, and leaves the patch test to you.

  • A stranger can book a full set tonight and arrive tomorrow having never been tested.
  • A form you remember to send is not a record, and a signed disclaimer is not a test.
  • A test done at somebody else's salon does not count, and nobody is checking.

What yours does

Refuses the booking, and says why.

  • She is told on the treatment, before she picks a day. Not at the end, after she has chosen a time and typed her name.
  • The patch test is a bookable appointment — and booking it is the record, so there is nothing to remember to write down.
  • Every client carries a date you can see, and the list tells you who lapses this month while they are still in your diary.
  • The hours and the lifetime are yours, per treatment, because insurers differ and the one that matters is the one you pay.

Booked In is not insurance and not legal advice: read your own policy wording and set these to match it. What it does is stop a booking being taken that your wording would not cover — on the free plan as well as the paid one, because a safety rule behind a paywall is not a safety rule.

Questions

Before you start

Do you take a cut of what I earn?
No. Your clients pay you, the way they already do — in person, however you take payment now. We never sit between you and your money, and the £10 is the only thing you pay us. Deposits taken through the page are being built; when they arrive the money will go straight to your own account.
Can two clients book the same slot?
No. The moment a time is taken it disappears for everyone else, even if two people are looking at your page at the same second.
Do I need a website already?
No — this is the website. You get a link to put in your Instagram bio, and that link is the whole thing your clients need.
Who owns my client list?
You do. Export it whenever you want, and if you leave we delete it. We will never message your clients or advertise anyone else to them.
What if I only work certain days in certain towns?
Set which days you cover which area and the calendar only ever offers those. A client in one town cannot book you on a day you are in another.
What if my insurer wants a different patch test window?
Then set yours to match it. The number of hours is set per treatment and how long a test stays valid is set once, both by you — we do not guess your policy's wording, and we would rather you checked it than took our default. Leave a treatment blank and it needs no test at all.
Does the patch test thing cost extra?
No. It is on the free plan and it always will be. A safety rule behind a paywall is not a safety rule.
I work from a room at home, not out of the car. Is this for me?
Yes. Tell us clients come to you and your page shows your address with a maps link, says the bit no address field holds — the buzzer, the parking, which door — and puts the location on the appointment your client saves to her phone. Tell us you travel instead and the page asks each client for her own address and hands it to you in your diary, as a link straight to maps. If you do both, your client picks when she books.
Can I cancel?
Any time, in two clicks. Your page keeps working until the end of the month you have paid for, and your bookings stay readable after that.
